\paragraph{Saved-probe evidence for accumulated phase drift.}
We analyse the existing 577 observations at spacing $0.125$, without integrating a new trajectory or shifting the physical scoring times. The primary window is $24\leq t\leq72$. After subtracting a least-squares linear trend, upward zero crossings are linearly interpolated and their times are fitted to an affine function of crossing index; the inverse slope estimates frequency. Both velocity and vorticity at $x=1.5,2.5,3.5$, $y=0$ are retained, with windows starting at 36 and 48, mean-only detrending, and both finer FD references as sensitivity checks. These finite-window estimates are descriptive, not confidence intervals or an unbounded-cylinder Strouhal validation.

Across all five random seeds and the midpoint controls, the primary-window no-layer frequency biases range from $-4.27\%$ to $-3.35\%$ over the six probe signals; including both references, windows and detrending variants expands this range to $-5.20\%$ to $-1.88\%$. The observed delays in Figure~\ref{fig:review-phase-probes} support accumulated phase drift as one component of the long-time mismatch. For the two no-layer midpoint rows, $2\pi(f-f_{\rm ref})48$ equals approximately $-2.22$ and $-2.10$ radians, a fitted rate-mismatch estimate rather than a measured full-field phase correction. The data do not support assigning all $70\%$-level field error to phase. The removed enrichments are localized at the \emph{outer} boundary, retained rank changes simultaneously, and no inner-wall separation-angle measurement was made. Thus an inner-wall underresolution--frequency-shift causal chain is not established.
